01

How this works

read this part

This is a workbench, not a robot. It does the research, ranking, drafting and scheduling for you. You do the clicking. Nothing here submits anything on your behalf, and that is a deliberate design decision rather than a missing feature — the reasons are below.

01

You enter your details, and they stay on this device

Name, city, state, and a contact email. This is stored in your own browser and is never transmitted — there is no server to transmit it to. It is used to build search links, fill in your letters, and remember your progress.

02

You search your own name on each broker site

This is the manual part, and there is no way around it. Each broker gets a "Check listing" button that opens their site with your name already in the search. You look at what comes back and decide whether that record is actually you.

The tool does not do this for you on purpose. Broker search pages are protected against automated access, and more importantly, a name and an age cannot reliably tell you apart from a stranger who shares them. Filing a deletion request against the wrong person's record is a real harm. Two seconds of your own eyes settles what no script can.

03

You submit the opt-out, or send a deletion letter

Most brokers have a web form: click through, fill it in, solve their CAPTCHA, click the link they email you. Roughly 20–60 seconds each once you have the rhythm.

Where a broker has a privacy contact, you can instead send a written deletion demand. Click Draft letter and the tool writes it, citing the statute your state actually gives you, and opens it in your own mail app. You review and hit send. It goes from your address, which also sidesteps the legal requirements that apply to anyone acting as your agent.

04

You mark it, and the tool remembers

Set each broker to Submitted when you've filed, and Confirmed once you've verified the listing is actually gone. Your progress is saved automatically in this browser — close the tab, come back next week, and it is all still there.

05

You download the follow-up calendar

Brokers ignore first requests routinely, and records repopulate months later. Section 5 generates a calendar file with reminders at 30, 45, 90 and 120 days for everything you've submitted. It imports into any calendar app and fires with this page closed.

What this cannot do for you

It cannot solve CAPTCHAs, complete phone verifications, or submit forms automatically. Those exist specifically to stop automation, and working around them would be both a terms-of-service violation and the kind of tool that gets abused for worse things than privacy.

It also cannot delete you. Opting out suppresses your listing on that broker's site. It does not touch public records, breach data already circulating, or private databases with no public interface. Anyone who tells you your data can be fully erased is selling something. Expect meaningful reduction in what a stranger can find by searching your name — not disappearance.

Realistically: budget an hour for the top 20 brokers, then about 20 minutes every few months for follow-ups and repopulated listings.

02

If you live in California, stop here

California residents — use the state platform instead

The state built a better version of this, and it is free

Under the Delete Act, the California Privacy Protection Agency runs DROP — the Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform. You submit one verified request and every data broker registered in California must delete your personal information and stop selling it.

This is not a courtesy request they can ignore. Since August 1, 2026, registered brokers are legally required to check DROP at least every 45 days, process what they find, and report status. Non-compliance carries penalties of $200 per request, per day. Several hundred thousand Californians have already used it.

It also covers cases this tool deliberately does not: a parent may submit for a child, and a family member may submit for an elderly relative.

Go do that first. It is strictly better than working through a list by hand. Then come back for the three gaps it leaves:

  • Brokers that never registered in California. DROP binds registrants only, and plenty of people-search sites simply do not register.
  • Verification. DROP will not show you whether a given listing actually came down. This tool tracks that per broker.
  • Everyone else. DROP is California residents only. Connecticut has committed to a comparable portal but not until 2028. Texas and Oregon publish broker registries with no unified deletion mechanism at all.

Your details

saved in this browser only

Use an email alias, not your main address. Several brokers add opt-out contacts to marketing lists. Proton Pass, SimpleLogin and iCloud Hide My Email all do this free. Street address is only needed for brokers that require it to match a record — leave it blank otherwise.

05

Follow-ups & backup

Reminders at 30, 45, 90 and 120 days for every broker you have marked submitted. After 120 days the escalation stops — indefinite automated resending reads as harassment and gets filtered as spam — and you get one final prompt to handle that broker by hand.

Your progress saves automatically in this browser as you go — close the tab and come back whenever. It is tied to this browser on this device, so it will not follow you to your phone, and clearing site data erases it. Use Export to keep a backup or move to another machine; the file contains the details you entered, so store it like any personal document.

This is free, and staying that way

No paid tier, nothing held back. If it saved you money or an afternoon, a coffee helps keep the broker list current when sites change their opt-out flows — which is the real ongoing cost here. Entirely optional.
